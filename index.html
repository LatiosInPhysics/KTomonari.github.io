Invitation

My research aims to formulate Metric-Affine Gravity from the perspective of Gauge Theories of Gravity. In General Relativity, spacetime is described by a pseudo-Riemannian manifold, and gravitational effects are encoded in its curvature. However, from the perspective of more general differential geometry, torsion and non-metricity can also be considered in addition to curvature. Theories describing gravity in terms of curvature and torsion have long been studied within the framework of Poincaré Gauge Theories of Gravity. (For an introduction to Gauge Theories of Gravity, see Blagojević and Hehl. For a comprehensive review of Metric-Affine Gravity, see Hehl et al..) On the other hand, how to incorporate non-metricity into the framework of Gauge Theories of Gravity remains an open question. One reason is that non-metricity vanishes when the internal metric is fixed to the Minkowski metric and the connection is restricted to be Lorentz-algebra-valued. This suggests that the internal symmetries and the structure of the connection need to be reconsidered in order to understand the Geometrical Trinity of Gravity (, in detail, see Beltrán Jiménez, Heisenberg, and Koivisto,Universe 5 (2019) 173, ) in a unified manner from the perspective of Gauge Theories of Gravity. My research explores how gravity based on non-metricity can be consistently incorporated into the framework of the Geometrical Trinity of Gravity using Gauge Theories of Gravity. The Introduction begins with a brief introduction to General Relativity, Gauge Theories of Gravity, and Metric-Affine Gravity, and then invites readers to explore my research.

Preprints

Kyosuke Tomonari, ``Schwarzschild spacetime in STEGR with information geometry'', in preparation for submission.
To be appeared in some preprint server (the manuscript currently very suffers from arXiv's moderation check).
Abstract:
We construct Schwarzschild spacetime within our information-geometric formulation of Symmetric Teleparallel Equivalent to General Relativity (STEGR) and investigate a possible probabilistic interpretation of its spatial geometry. First, after reviewing $\alpha$-geometry based on Markov embeddings and introducing STEGR as an information-geometric theory, we reformulate this theory using the Arnowitt-Deser-Misner formalism, in which spacetime is foliated by three-dimensional spatial leaves endowed with Hessian structures. In this formulation, we introduce the three-dimensional probability simplex $\mathcal{S}_{3}$ to provide a statistical description of the spatial leaves. We then formulate conditions for preserving the Markov invariance of $(0,2)$- and $(0,3)$-tensors, which correspond, respectively, to the Fisher metric and the Amari-Chentsov tensor in a specific case during ADM evolution, together with field equations describing spatial leaves and their time evolution. Solving this system in the static vacuum case, we reconstruct Schwarzschild spacetime. In particular, we derive an explicit Hessian potential yielding a positive-definite spatial metric in the Schwarzschild exterior region. Finally, we discuss the relations among the underlying geometries and the possibility of endowing the spatial leaves with a probabilistic character, and derive the explicit form of the Bregman divergence of the spatial leaves of Schwarzschild spacetime.
